## Likely questions from Roland — and your crisp answers

**"Why now? Why couldn't this exist 12 months ago?"**
Six months ago agents couldn't complete a purchase. Now they can — OpenAI ACP, Google's shopping protocol, Visa and Mastercard agent payments all went live. Optimization only matters once the agent is the buyer. That moment just happened.

**"Isn't this a feature, not a company?"**
A monitoring feature would be. We're a closed loop — simulate, fix, publish, measure — across every major agent and platform, compounding into a proprietary content→behavior→revenue dataset. That dataset is the company.

**"What stops Shopify or OpenAI from doing this themselves?"**
Platforms optimize for *themselves and the buyer*, not the merchant. Merchants need a neutral advocate that works across *all* agents at once. That's structurally not Shopify's or OpenAI's job — it's ours.

**"What's the moat?"**
Every simulation and fix is a labeled datapoint linking content → agent behavior → revenue. The more stores we connect, the better our predictions and the harder we are to copy. First-mover data advantage in a brand-new category.

**"How real is the win-rate lift? How do you measure it?"**
We stage real buying sessions across agents, measure whether the merchant's product gets chosen vs. competitors, apply fixes, and re-run. Lift is the change in agent win rate, tracked through to revenue. [Have one concrete pilot number ready.]

**"Walk me through traction."**
Paying pilots with real catalogs: Dua Flavours (£10K+ MRR online) with écentic as the optimization layer; Avatid as a further design partner; enterprise pilots in discussion via Plug and Play (eBay, Carrefour, Inditex). Early but real — live merchants, real lift. [Be honest about scale; don't inflate.]

**"Go-to-market and CAC?"**
Shopify App Store as primary channel — one-click install, self-serve, app-store discovery → ~4.6M merchants in reach at low CAC. Design-partner pilots now for proof; enterprise via partners.

**"Unit economics?"**
~$6K blended ACV, ~85% gross margins, costs scale with API usage not headcount. Land self-serve, expand by usage and tier.

**"Who are your competitors?"**
Answer-engine optimization tools (e.g. Profound) and legacy Shopify SEO apps — they monitor and advise. None auto-fix listings for AI agents or own the transaction-level data. Auto-fix + data corpus is our wedge.

**"Why this team specifically?"**
We've been Shopify merchants since 2020 — we lived this problem. We've built a startup before, we ship fast, and we cover vision, engineering and sales across the three of us.

**"What do you need beyond money?"**
Global GTM beyond CEE (our customers are in the US/Western Europe), network intros, and senior hiring. This is where I'd want Day One hands-on. [Then ask how they'd help.]

**"How much are you raising, at what valuation?"**
€750K for ~18 months of runway. [See valuation note below — be ready for pushback.]

**"What are the milestones for this money?"**
Month 3: first 10 design partners. Month 6: 150 paying merchants. Month 12: €450K ARR + 2 enterprise pilots live → seed-ready. Use of funds: 50% GTM + Shopify App Store, 30% catalog activation + runway, 20% engineering + advisory.

## The valuation conversation (your likeliest friction point)

At **€750K on €7.5M pre-money**, even taking the whole round Day One ends up with under 10% — below the 10–20% a concentrated lead usually wants. Expect Roland to push on price or suggest a larger round.

**How to handle it:**
- Don't get defensive. Frame the number as reflecting founder-market fit + early traction + category timing, and say you're open to discussing structure.
- Have a fallback in your pocket: e.g. **€7.5M post-money** (= exactly 10% for the round, €6.75M pre) reads cleaner and more standard. *(Only present it as post-money if your submitted deck didn't explicitly say "pre-money.")*
- A SAFE with a cap is a flexible alternative if they want to defer a hard number.
- Bottom line: know your floor before you walk in, and don't negotiate against yourself in the room.

## Do / Don't

**Do**
- Lead with the *logic* of a billion-euro outcome — let the math imply the unicorn. Conviction reads better than "I want a unicorn."
- Make "right hand" concrete: bring 2–3 specific asks and turn it into a two-way conversation.
- Be honest that you're running a process with genuine interest, and that Day One is the partner you'd *most want to lead*. Wanted, but not guaranteed.

**Don't**
- Don't say "you're my one and only." It removes your leverage and the competitive tension that makes funds move.
- Don't imply investor interest that isn't real — VCs talk; it's easy to catch and it's fatal to trust.
- Don't try to close today. Earn the next meeting.
